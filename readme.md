# Ctruh Landing Page Flow (Simple Version)

One flow, reused for every (industry + use case) combo. Only the words and examples change page to page — the structure stays the same.

  

1\. Header

Just the logo and one button. No menu, no links to the rest of the site. The only job of this page is to get one action.

CTA: the persistent button — same one throughout.

2\. Hero (top of page)

*   A headline that states the outcome, not the tech (e.g. "Let customers try it on before they buy")
    
*   One line under it explaining what it is and how it works
    
*   One button
    
*   Both videos live here:
    
*   The explainer video is the main visual — human in professional ambiance talking about the product and fading out as the video shows the product actually working while the person is speaking in the background (Human & human voice is imp. No AI)
    
*   The Shark Tank clip sits next to it as a small "As Seen On" badge with a play icon — clicking it opens a short highlight clip, not the full episode
    

CTA: "Start Free" or "Book a Demo" — whichever fits the visitor's likely size.

3\. Who it's for

A quick gut-check for the visitor: "If you're \[doing X problem in this industry\], this is for you." Short, 2–3 lines. Its only job is to make the person think "yes, that's me" and keep scrolling.  
  
Add any relevant content + keywords here for the (industry + use case) combo - answering how it is for them.

CTA: soft, low-commitment — "See how it works" (scrolls down, no form).

4\. What you get

3–4 short benefit statements. Not features — outcomes. E.g. "Fewer returns," "No 3D team needed," "Live in your store the same day."

CTA: "Try it with your own product."

  5. Client Testimonials  —  (section breaker)  

A quote or two from real customers, ideally in the same industry as the page. (Flagging again — this is the piece you're currently missing and it's worth getting before launch.)

CTA: "Read more customer stories" or "Get results like this — start free."

6\. How it works

4 simple steps, shown visually: Upload → AI generates the 3D/AR → Customize → Publish. This answers "how do I actually get this."

CTA: "Start your first upload — it's free."

7\. See it in action

Real examples of this exact use case — a short clip or a few images, not a wall of screenshots. This is the "prove it" moment.

CTA: "See more examples" or "Try this on your product."

8\. Studio or Enterprise

A short two-column pick: "Doing this yourself? Try Studio." / "Want us to set it up? Book a call." Helps the right visitor hit the right button.

CTA: this section's whole job IS the CTA — "Start Free" on one side, "Book a Demo" on the other.

  9. Proof  —  (section breaker)  

*   Shark Tank story, full clip
    
*   Awards, funding, patents — whatever's current and cleared to use
    
*   Any hard numbers you're confident are still accurate
    

  

CTA: "Watch the full pitch" + a repeated primary button.

10\. FAQ

4–6 questions that remove hesitation: cost, setup time, technical skill needed, does it work with their existing store/site.

CTA: "Still unsure? Talk to us."

11\. Final CTA

Same button, same promise, one more time. No new information here — just the last nudge.

CTA: primary button, repeated.

12\. Footer

Logo, one line, legal. Small link back to the main site for anyone not ready to convert.

  

The one rule that matters most: every industry+use case page keeps this exact order. Only the headline, the "who it's for" line, the proof examples, and the video content change.
